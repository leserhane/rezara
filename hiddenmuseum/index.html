Struck in Paris

The coinage of Sultan Moulay al-Hassan I — five pieces, one vitrine, and the reform that tried to give Morocco a single, modern currency.

01 / 05

A reform in metal

In the 1880s, Moulay al-Hassan I set out to replace a patchwork of foreign and local coins with a currency bearing Morocco’s own name. The new pieces — silver for the treasury, bronze for the market — were ordered from the most precise mints in Europe.

02 / 05

“Struck in Paris”

Look at the centre of the largest coin. The calligraphy reads ضرب بباريس — struck in Paris. A sultan’s coin announcing, without apology, where it was made: modernity was a thing you could commission.

03 / 05

Counting in another calendar

On the reverse, عام ١٣٠٦ — the year 1306 of the Hijra, 1888–89 in the Gregorian calendar. The numerals are set in European type, the word for “year” in flowing Maghrebi script. Two worlds on a single face.

04 / 05

Change for the souk

The smallest bronze in the case was the one that travelled furthest — from palm to palm, stall to stall. Its warm red patina is the residue of a century of ordinary lives.

05 / 05

The last silver

Dated 1311 — 1893–94 — this worn silver piece belongs to the final year of the reign. Its softened edges are a record of use, and of how quickly a reform can become simply money.

Stories

Every object is a small biography.

Scroll to travel · select a coin to zoom

  1. Bronze · Paris · AH 1306

    The widest voice

    The largest bronze of the series, made to be seen across a market stall.

    Its border of eight-pointed stars echoes the tile work of Fez — a European machine asked to speak in Moroccan ornament.

  2. Silver · AH 1311

    The last silver

    Minted in the final year of Moulay al-Hassan I’s reign.

    Worn smooth at the rim, the date still stands proud: 1311. Silver is soft; every hand that held it took a little away.

  3. AH 1310

    A darker skin

    Same design, different century of weather.

    Patina is not damage but biography — oxygen, sweat and time, written as a thin dark film over the metal.

  4. Bronze · Paris · AH 1306

    A border of stars

    A ring of arabesques that frames the date like a doorway.

    Look closely and each star is slightly different — the engraver’s hand survives even inside an industrial die.

  5. Bronze · AH 1306

    Change for the souk

    The smallest coin here, and the busiest.

    Small change is where a currency becomes real. Bread, mint tea, a ride across the river: this is the coin that paid for them.

  6. Bimetallic blanks · today

    Before the strike

    A coin begins as a blank — a ring and a core, pressed together, waiting for a face.

    A single strike of the press, in a fraction of a second, gives the blank its design, its edge and its value.

  7. Bimetallic blanks · today

    Two metals, one coin

    Gold-coloured ring, silver-coloured heart — the hardest coin to counterfeit is the one made of two.

    The two alloys respond differently to machines that test them, turning metallurgy itself into a security feature.

Held up to the light.
